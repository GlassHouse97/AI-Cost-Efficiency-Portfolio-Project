MRU AI value Portfolio Project

Intermediate · About 12 to 16 hours for core analysis and dashboard · Excel, Power Query, SQL, BI, or code

Marketing-R-Us (MRU) is a fictional marketing company using the fictional ChatABC AI platform. Evaluate whether the estimated value of staff time saved justifies AI spending, then explain how prompt volume, model prices, rework, and campaign economics relate. All people, clients, prices, and results are synthetic. This dataset is an analytical exercise, not evidence about a real provider or a causal experiment.

This standalone package contains mru-portfolio-project.xlsx with four raw data sheets, all four matching CSVs, a learner brief, column guide, metric formulas, and a deliverable checklist. Use the workbook or the CSV files: their source records are identical, so importing both versions of a table would double-count the data. Neither format contains completed answers. Open START_HERE.html for the complete offline guide. You can finish this exercise without visiting a hosted dashboard or any author files. The completed portfolio demo and public generator, if viewed separately, contain spoilers.

Campaign 1 and Campaign 2 are the focal comparison. Campaign 3 and Campaign 4 provide additional context. There are 240 unique completed tasks, 60 per campaign, and the same eight fictional employees across Content, Creative, Marketing Operations, and Analytics. CampaignChat supports planning, CopyStudio writing, DataFlow data work, and InsightDesk summaries. Every task uses one product and one model. Every prompt belongs to its task's campaign.

Value all staff time at $50 per hour. Actual hours include preparation, prompting, task setup, checking, and corrections. Rework is already inside actual hours. Baseline and actual hours cover the same completed task at acceptable quality. Net AI value estimates staff capacity value after AI fees; it is not payroll cash savings.

Prepare the data in this order

  1. Keep raw files unchanged. Record file row counts and inspect column types. Parse dates as YYYY-MM-DD, tokens as nonnegative integers, rates and hours as numeric values, and Yes/No fields as categorical observations.
  2. Remove exact repeated records using each file's unique key, then assert that the remaining keys are unique. If the same key has conflicting values, investigate instead of keeping an arbitrary row. Trim and standardize department labels to Content, Creative, Marketing Operations, and Analytics.
  3. Validate that every prompt links to one work item and that its campaign_id agrees with the task. Confirm exactly one model and one product per task, and exactly one price row per model.
  4. Join ai_usage to model_pricing on model. Calculate separate input and output costs for each prompt. Count one row as one employee prompt. Retain precision; no precomputed costs are supplied.
  5. Aggregate prompt rows to work_item_id before joining task hours. Sum input tokens, output tokens, input cost, output cost, total cost, and prompt count. You may also summarize correction and followup counts. Carry the task's single product and model through to this task-level table.
  6. Join this one-row-per-task summary to the cleaned work_log. Validate one resulting row per task. Calculate hours saved, time value, and net AI value once per task. Never sum task hours from a prompt-level join.
  7. Group the enriched task table by campaign, department, product, and model as needed. Compute ROI and cost per prompt from summed numerators and denominators. Do not average task ratios.
  8. For whole-campaign results, first aggregate labor hours and AI costs to one row per campaign, then join one campaign financial row. Subtract other spending, actual hours × $50, and AI cost from revenue. Never multiply revenue by the number of tasks or prompts.
  9. Separately retain prompt-level usage_date for daily usage and spending. Do not substitute task completion date for the billing-style usage timeline.
  10. Reconcile totals, run the baseline sensitivity exercise, and build the dashboard and findings page.

You can do this in any familiar tool. In Excel, open the supplied workbook and save your own working copy, or import each CSV as a table using Data → From Text/CSV and save as .xlsx. Use only one format of each input. Use PivotTables or Power Query to aggregate prompts before connecting task hours. In SQL or code, use a grouped prompt summary as the intermediate table. No particular stack is required.

Analytical questions

  1. For each campaign, what are input and output tokens, their separate costs, total AI cost, prompt count, cost per prompt, hours saved, net AI value, and ROI?
  2. How do the focal campaigns C001 and C002 differ in product and model mix? Is spending associated with token volume, model prices, repeated work, or several of these?
  3. Within similar task types, what patterns appear in rework, correction prompts, changed briefs, and template use? Avoid employee rankings and do not claim causation from observed associations.
  4. What is each whole campaign's result after other spending, human labor value, and AI fees? Explain why campaign profit or loss differs from net AI value.
  5. How do net AI values change when baseline hours are 20% lower or 20% higher? Which conclusions are sensitive to that assumption?
  6. What two or three practical actions would you recommend, and what evidence and caveats support each?

Dashboard views and filter meaning

Build up to three views: Company overview, Campaign comparison, and Workflow investigation. Show input and output tokens and cost, baseline versus actual hours, net AI value, and aggregate ROI. Filters for campaign, department, product, and model are meaningful for task-level operating metrics because each task has one product and model.

Keep the financial panel explicitly labeled Whole-campaign financial results. Campaign selection can choose which complete campaigns to show. Department, product, model, and prompt-date filters must not change full campaign profit or whole-campaign ROI. Do not allocate revenue or other campaign spending to those filtered subsets without an explicit allocation method, which this exercise does not require. If you show a filtered task ROI, label it Selected tasks ROI so it is not confused with whole-campaign ROI.

Use usage_date for daily spending. A date-limited prompt chart includes only the prompts in that date range; it cannot inherit complete task hours or revenue as if they were earned on those prompt dates. Keep task-level baseline, actual hours, net value, and ROI based on complete tasks. Clearly state which visuals each filter changes; a date filter may affect the usage chart alone.

The company total should be derived from summed costs and hours, and the same complete campaign should agree across every view. A weighted aggregate ROI can differ from an average of campaign ROIs; use the ratio of totals.

Portfolio Project metric formulas

Use $50 per hour for every employee. Use the two supplied fixed fictional model prices: Lite input 10 and output 30 USD per million; Pro input 40 and output 100 USD per million.

Prompt level

After joining each prompt to exactly one model price row:

Metric Formula
total_tokensinput_tokens + output_tokens
input_cost_usdinput_tokens × input_rate_usd_per_million ÷ 1,000,000
output_cost_usdoutput_tokens × output_rate_usd_per_million ÷ 1,000,000
ai_cost_usdinput_cost_usd + output_cost_usd
Prompt countCount distinct usage_id after cleaning; one row is one employee prompt.

Retain full precision during calculations. If costs are stored, retain six decimal places. Display USD to cents and do not round each prompt to cents before summing. Use integer token counts and the supplied whole-dollar rates, so the costs are traceable to their inputs.

Task level

First aggregate input tokens, output tokens, input cost, output cost, total cost, and prompt count to one row per task, then join work_log. Carry each task's single model and product through the aggregation. Include all its prompts when comparing complete task hours.

Metric Formula
hours_savedbaseline_hours − actual_hours
time_value_usdhours_saved × 50
net_ai_value_usdtime_value_usd − ai_cost_usd

Actual hours already include rework. Do not add it again. A task's 1.5 actual hours remain 1.5 hours even if it has 20 prompts.

Group metrics

For each selected campaign, department, product, model, or task type, sum the appropriate task or prompt numerators and denominators first.

Metric Formula
AI ROI percentagesum(net_ai_value_usd) ÷ sum(ai_cost_usd) × 100
Average cost per promptsum(ai_cost_usd) ÷ sum(prompt_count)
Rework share percentagesum(rework_hours) ÷ sum(actual_hours) × 100
Correction share percentagecount(Correction prompts) ÷ count(all prompts) × 100
Input spending share percentagesum(input_cost_usd) ÷ sum(ai_cost_usd) × 100
Output spending share percentagesum(output_cost_usd) ÷ sum(ai_cost_usd) × 100

Show N/A if a denominator is zero. Never average row ROI or row cost per prompt. ROI can be below −100% when human effort also increases. In Excel, divide numerator by denominator and apply Percentage formatting; do not multiply the already formatted fraction by 100 a second time.

Compare template use and changed briefs within comparable task types and campaigns where possible. Report group task counts alongside averages or shares. These descriptive relationships do not isolate causal effects.

Whole campaign financial result

Aggregate actual hours and AI cost to one row per campaign before joining campaigns.csv:

campaign_result_usd = campaign_revenue_usd − other_campaign_cost_usd − (campaign_actual_hours × 50) − campaign_ai_cost_usd

Revenue and other costs each enter the formula once. Other spending excludes staff time and AI fees. Net AI value compares AI-assisted effort to the estimated no-AI baseline. Campaign result compares revenue to total stated costs. They answer different questions and need separate labels.

Campaign filters may select whole campaigns. Department, product, model, and prompt-date filters do not change the whole-campaign financial panel or whole-campaign ROI. If you provide ROI for a filtered task cohort, label it Selected tasks ROI.

Baseline sensitivity at minus and plus 20 percent

Change baseline hours only; keep actual hours, AI fees, the $50 hourly rate, revenue, and other costs fixed.

Scenario Net AI value formula
Baseline 20% lower((baseline_hours × 0.8) − actual_hours) × 50 − ai_cost_usd
Stated baseline(baseline_hours − actual_hours) × 50 − ai_cost_usd
Baseline 20% higher((baseline_hours × 1.2) − actual_hours) × 50 − ai_cost_usd

Calculate these at the task level and sum, or apply the same factors to consistently scoped sums. Show the three net-value results for each focal campaign, report whether the sign changes, and explain what that implies about reliance on the baseline estimate. Campaign result does not change in this test because it uses actual hours rather than baseline hours.

Worked example from tokens to value

This illustration is independent of the campaign answers. A Lite task has 20 prompts, each with 10,000 input tokens and 5,000 output tokens. Input cost per prompt is 10,000 × 10 ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.10. Output cost is 5,000 × 30 ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.15. Each prompt costs $0.25, so the task costs $5.00.

Aggregate first: 20 prompts contain 200,000 input and 100,000 output tokens. The task calculation (200,000 × 10 + 100,000 × 30) ÷ 1,000,000 also equals $5.00. Total tokens are 300,000.

The no-AI estimate is 2 hours and actual time is 1.5 hours, including review and rework. Hours saved are 0.5; time value is $25; net AI value is $20; ROI is 400%. Joining all 20 prompts directly to task hours and summing would wrongly create 30 actual hours. Instead, aggregate prompts first and count the task's 1.5 hours once.

Portfolio Project deliverable checklist

Deliver your work

  • An interactive dashboard with up to three views: Company overview, Campaign comparison, and Workflow investigation.
  • Preparation steps in a reproducible query, script, workbook, or clear tool instructions, including duplicate handling and department standardization.
  • A short findings page explaining the focal campaigns, costs, effort, workflow patterns, full campaign results, and baseline sensitivity.
  • Two or three supported recommendations, each tied to evidence and accompanied by a relevant caveat.
  • Definitions and assumptions visible to the reviewer, including the $50 hourly rate, fictional fixed prices, row meanings, and filter behavior.
  • A brief disclosure of any outside AI help and your own role in the work.

Validate the source and transformations

  • Check unique IDs in every cleaned table and document raw versus cleaned record counts.
  • Confirm 240 unique tasks, 60 per campaign, and eight employees across four standardized departments.
  • Confirm every prompt matches exactly one task and its campaign.
  • Confirm each task uses one product and model, and every model maps to one unique price row.
  • Confirm tokens are nonnegative integers and required hours and prices are populated.
  • Confirm actual and rework hours are nonnegative, with rework no greater than actual hours.
  • Derive prompt charges from token counts and rates, with six decimal places if stored and rounding only for display.
  • Aggregate prompts before joining hours; prove task row counts and total hours are unchanged by that join.
  • Aggregate task financial measures to one campaign row before adding revenue and other spending.
  • Reconcile AI costs and token totals between prompt, task, campaign, and company summaries.

Validate the dashboard and interpretation

  • Show input and output tokens and their separate costs, prompt count, cost per prompt, baseline and actual hours, net AI value, and aggregate ROI.
  • Use ratios of totals and show N/A for zero denominators.
  • Provide meaningful campaign, department, product, and model filters for task-level operating measures.
  • Label the full campaign financial panel and keep department, product, model, and prompt-date filters from changing whole-campaign profit or whole-campaign ROI.
  • Use usage_date for daily spending and explain that its date filter applies to prompt measures only.
  • Show the same whole-campaign totals consistently across views.
  • Distinguish net AI value from overall campaign result and estimated staff capacity from payroll cash savings.
  • Compare similar tasks when discussing rework, corrections, brief changes, and templates; report sample counts and avoid causal claims or employee rankings.
  • Recalculate net AI value with baselines 20% lower and higher, then explain whether the conclusions change.
  • Make USD, token, hour, and percentage units clear and preserve negative values.
  • If you publish your completed solution, label it clearly as a completed demo containing spoilers and keep raw learner downloads separate.

Evidence for your findings page

State the decision MRU faces, briefly explain how you priced prompts and protected task hours from duplication, summarize the results you calculated, and give two or three concrete actions. Include the sensitivity result and the limits of an observational synthetic dataset. Support claims with your own numbers; this learner package does not supply campaign conclusions.
